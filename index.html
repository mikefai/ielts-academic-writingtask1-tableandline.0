Module 1 • Foundations

The 4-Paragraph Golden Formula

In IELTS Task 1, you must write at least 150 words in 20 minutes. Do not invent your own essay structure! Follow this proven 4-paragraph skeleton every time:

Paragraph 1 1 Sentence • ~25 words

Introduction (Paraphrase the Question)

Tell the reader what the graph/table shows. Rewrite the question prompt in your own words.

Formula: The [chart type] illustrates [what] in [where] between [year] and [year].
Paragraph 2 2-3 Sentences • ~35-45 words

The Overview (Crucial for Band 6 & 7)

Highlight 2 to 3 main general trends (e.g. highest, lowest, biggest increase). NO exact numbers here!

Formula: Overall, it is clear that [Main trend 1]. Additionally, [Main trend 2].
Paragraph 3 3-4 Sentences • ~50-60 words

Body Paragraph 1 (Group A Details)

Describe the first group of data with accurate numbers, units, start values, end values, and key changes.

Focus: Upward trends OR top categories with exact figures.
Paragraph 4 3-4 Sentences • ~50-60 words

Body Paragraph 2 (Group B Details & Comparisons)

Describe the remaining categories, compare them directly to Body 1, and report remaining key data points.

Focus: Downward/fluctuating trends OR bottom categories.
Why are students stuck at Band 5.0?
  • No Overview: They forget paragraph 2 or put specific numbers inside the overview.
  • Listing data like a phone book: "In 2000 it was 10. In 2005 it was 12. In 2010 it was 15." (Boring & low vocabulary).
  • Preposition Mistakes: Saying "increased at 10%" instead of "increased by 10% to 40%".
  • Under 150 words: Direct penalty on Task Achievement.

Mixed Task Type (Table + Line Graph):

"When you get both a Line Graph and a Table in the exam: dedicate Body Paragraph 1 to the Line Graph and Body Paragraph 2 to the Table. Do not mix them in the same paragraph!"

Graphic 1 (Line): Trend Over Time

Focus on upward/downward slope, turning points, and trajectory over years.

Graphic 2 (Table): Categories & Gaps

Focus on rankings, top vs lowest disparities, and categorical comparisons.

Module 4 • Line Graph Lexicon

How to Describe Trends (Simple & Accurate)

Band 5 students only use "went up" and "went down". To get Band 6.5 or 7, you need precise verbs, adjectives, and adverbs.

Upward Movement (↑)
Verbs:

rose, increased, climbed, surged (fast), peaked at (highest point)

Nouns:

a rise, an increase, an upward trend, a surge

"Renewables soared significantly from 15 to 85 TWh."
Downward Movement (↓)
Verbs:

fell, decreased, dropped, plummeted (steep drop), declined

Nouns:

a decline, a drop, a downturn, a reduction

"Coal production plummeted dramatically to 10 TWh."
Fluctuation / Flat (∼)
Verbs:

fluctuated, remained steady, plateaued, levelled off

Adverbs:

gradually, steadily, sharply, moderately

"Gas consumption fluctuated moderately between 40 and 58 TWh."

The Preposition Master Table (Stop Losing Easy Marks!)

AT = Exact Point "Started at 15 TWh"
"Peaked at 85 TWh"
TO = Finish Number "Rose to 85 TWh" (Destination value)
BY = Difference / Gap "Increased by 70 TWh" (85 - 15 = 70)
BETWEEN / AND "Between 2000 and 2020"
Module 5 • Table Strategy

How to Master Table Questions Without Panicking

Tables have many numbers. You do NOT need to write every single number! If you write all numbers, you will run out of time and receive Band 5 for Task Achievement.

1

Find the Extremes

Always identify the highest and lowest numbers in the first and final years.

2

Group by Pattern

Put items that increased rapidly in Body 1. Put items that decreased or stayed flat in Body 2.

3

Calculate Easy Gaps

Use words like "double", "quadruple", "two-fold increase" instead of repeating percentages.

3 Ready-to-Use Table Sentences:
  • Rankings: "Browsing the internet saw the most substantial growth, ranking as the most popular activity by 2020 at 92%."
  • Opposite extremes: "While phone calls experienced a slight decline from 100% to 88%, game usage nearly quadrupled to 68%."
  • Lowest group: "In contrast, navigation features accounted for the smallest proportion throughout the period, despite rising to 48%."

Module 7 • Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

Band 6.0 vs Band 6.5 Full Comparison

Below is the complete essay for the Electricity Generation Line Graph. Look sentence by sentence to see exactly what transforms a basic Band 6.0 into a strong Band 6.5+!

Sentence 1: Introduction Goal: Paraphrase topic + timeframe
Band 6.0 Version Simple paraphrase

"The line graph shows how much electricity was made from three fuel types in a European country from 2000 to 2020."

Teacher Note: Good, but words like "shows" and "made" are very simple, and unit of measurement (TWh) is missing.
Band 6.5 Version Academic & Complete

"The line graph illustrates electricity generation measured in terawatt hours (TWh) across three energy sources in an EU nation over a 20-year period starting in 2000."

Why it's 6.5: Uses precise academic vocabulary (illustrates, generation), includes the exact unit (TWh), and upgrades timeframe phrasing.
Sentence 2 & 3: The Overview Goal: Report general trajectory without numbers
Band 6.0 Version Basic Trend

"Overall, electricity from renewables increased a lot and became the biggest source. On the other hand, coal decreased and gas went up and down."

Teacher Note: Clear overview (secures Band 6), but phrases like "increased a lot" and "went up and down" are informal.
Band 6.5 Version Sophisticated Synthesis

"Overall, it is noticeable that renewable energy experienced substantial growth, overtaking all other sources by 2020. In stark contrast, coal witnessed a dramatic downward trend, while natural gas showed moderate fluctuations."

Why it's 6.5: Cohesive markers ("In stark contrast"), compound complex structure ("overtaking all other sources..."), and formal collocation ("witnessed a dramatic downward trend").
Sentence 4 to 6: Body Paragraph 1 Goal: Describe Upward & Mid trends with data
Band 6.0 Version Repetitive data list

"In 2000, renewables started at only 15 TWh. Then, it grew steadily and reached around 50 TWh in 2010. By 2020, it peaked at 85 TWh. Meanwhile, natural gas stood at 40 TWh in 2000, then rose to 58 TWh in 2010 before declining to 45 TWh at the end."

Teacher Note: Accurate data, but uses mechanical connector pattern ("Then... then... By...").
Band 6.5 Version Fluid, Connected Data Flow

"Looking at the growing sectors, renewable energy began at a modest 15 TWh in 2000 before surging dramatically by 70 TWh to reach a peak of 85 TWh at the end of the period. Similarly, natural gas generation, which started at 40 TWh, climbed to a high of 58 TWh in 2010 prior to settling at 45 TWh in 2020."

Why it's 6.5: Employs participle clauses ("before surging...", "prior to settling...") and calculates the exact increase ("by 70 TWh").
Sentence 7 to 9: Body Paragraph 2 Goal: Describe Downward trend & compare
Band 6.0 Version Isolated points

"Regarding coal, it was the highest in 2000 at 65 TWh. However, it decreased quickly in the next 20 years. By 2020, coal dropped to only 10 TWh, which was the lowest figure in the whole chart."

Teacher Note: Clear and grammatically sound, but misses complex comparative linking phrases.
Band 6.5 Version Direct Comparative Integration

"In sharp contrast, coal was initially the dominant fuel source at 65 TWh in 2000. However, its output plummeted continuously over the two decades, finishing at a negligible 10 TWh in 2020. Consequently, coal went from being the leading electricity generator to the least utilized source by the conclusion of the survey."

Why it's 6.5: High-scoring lexical items ("dominant fuel source", "plummeted continuously", "negligible", "least utilized") and clear comparative overview logic.
Module 8 • Examiner Criteria

Band 5.0 vs 6.0 vs 6.5 vs 7.0 Matrix

Here is what the IELTS examiner evaluates across the 4 scoring criteria:

Criterion Band 5.0 (Current) Band 6.0 Band 6.5 Band 7.0 (Target)
Task Achievement (TA) No clear overview; only raw numbers copied without main features. Presents an overview; reports main trends but some data mechanical. Clear, highlighted overview with well-selected data points. Comprehensive overview; clear highlight of key trends and proportional shifts.
Coherence & Cohesion (CC) Sentences disconnected; basic linkers ("And", "Then") overused. Information arranged coherently; uses basic connectors ("Meanwhile", "However"). Logical progression; varied transitions ("In sharp contrast", "Prior to"). Seamless paragraphing and effortless flow across comparison clauses.
Lexical Resource (LR) Repetitive words ("went up", "went down", "shows"). Adequate vocabulary; some attempts at "increased", "decreased". Good range of collocations ("surged dramatically", "modest start"). Precise academic lexicon, flexible collocations, and zero repetition.
Grammar & Accuracy (GRA) Frequent preposition errors ("at 10%"); simple sentences only. Mix of simple and compound sentences; few minor errors. Uses complex sentences (relative clauses, participle clauses). Wide range of error-free complex structures with perfect prepositions.
